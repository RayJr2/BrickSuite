My Inventory

My Inventory is the working view of the loose parts you own and can use. Unlike the Parts Catalog, which describes part designs, My Inventory records the actual part/color combinations in your Workspace, their quantities, condition, ownership, and physical Storage locations.

Browsing and Finding Inventory

The inventory table shows the part image, part number, name, category, color, quantity, Storage location, condition, ownership, and an Actions menu.

Use Search to find inventory by part number or name. Category, Color, and Storage filters can be used independently or together to narrow the result. Choose Search after selecting the criteria you want to apply.

The message above the table identifies the currently displayed result range. The summary below the table shows the number of inventory records matching the current criteria, while the pager at the lower right moves through larger result sets.

Inventory record vs. quantity: An inventory record represents a particular part/color/condition/ownership/location combination. Its Qty column tells you how many pieces that record currently represents.

Adding Loose Parts

Choose Add Part... when you want to add loose pieces directly from My Inventory. Select the part, color, Storage location, condition, ownership type, and quantity.

In this example, BrickSuite is ready to add ten used, owned 3001 — Brick 2 x 4 pieces in Black to Cabinet Bricks / Basic Bricks / Black.

The Show all colors option makes the complete color list available when the normal known colors for the selected part do not include the color you need. Keep Open is useful when entering several parts in succession.

You can also begin this workflow from Parts Catalog by choosing Add to Inventory for a catalog part.

Editing an Inventory Record

Choose Edit from the row's Actions menu when you need to maintain the inventory attributes or quantity.

The example shows four used, owned 3002 — Brick 2 x 3 pieces in Blue. The Edit workflow lets you maintain the color, condition, ownership, and quantity associated with the selected inventory record.

Edit does not relocate inventory. If the physical pieces are moving to a different Storage location, use Move instead. This lets BrickSuite preserve the movement in the inventory history.

Moving Inventory

Choose Move from the Actions menu when pieces are physically relocated. BrickSuite shows the current Storage location and available quantity, then asks for the destination and the number of pieces to move.

Here, all four available 3002 pieces are being prepared to move from Shelf Top / Bricks to Cabinet Bricks / Basic Bricks / Other Colors.

You do not have to move the entire available quantity. Enter a smaller quantity to perform a partial move. BrickSuite keeps the remaining pieces in the source record and transfers the selected quantity to the destination as appropriate.

Inventory History

Choose History to see how a selected part/color inventory has changed over time. History is useful for answering questions such as where pieces came from, when they were imported, and where they were moved.

The example history for 3002 — Brick 2 x 3 / Blue shows several CSV imports followed by a Move event. Each row can record the date and time, event type, quantity, source and destination, condition, ownership, reference, and details or notes.

References such as the Rebrickable CSV filename make imported inventory traceable to the operation that created it.

Edit, Move, and History

These three operations have different purposes:

Edit
  Maintain the inventory record and quantity.

Move
  Physically relocate pieces and record the movement.

History
  Review the events that produced the current inventory state.

Using the dedicated Move workflow rather than treating a location change as a simple edit is what allows BrickSuite to maintain the digital history of the physical inventory.

Importing Inventory

Choose Import CSV to preview and import an owned-parts CSV exported from Rebrickable. Imported rows become loose inventory in the selected Workspace and are recorded in inventory history.

See Rebrickable Import for the complete preview, Storage selection, and import workflow.

Lost Inventory

If pieces that BrickSuite expects to be in inventory cannot be physically located, use Lost Inventory... rather than deleting or artificially adjusting the record. The Lost/Found workflow preserves what happened and supports returning a piece when it is found later.

See Lost / Found for details.

Inventory and Builds

Build workflows can allocate, pull, return, or otherwise change the availability of loose inventory. BrickSuite keeps those operations connected to the Build workflow so the current inventory state remains traceable.

See Builds, Allocate Available, and Missing Parts for the Build side of inventory management.
